the brain, and what the reward does to it

which part answers: green pulses are the letter arriving from the receptors, cyan is the sparse code crossing the calyx, amber is the vote leaving for the mouthparts. the vote strip at the bottom is all 27 output cells — the tallest bar is the answer, and the second-tallest is what it beat.
what glows for sugar: a gold burst sweeps up from the taste organ through the subesophageal zone and along PAM/DAN onto the mushroom body, then an expanding gold ring appears on the row that earned the credit. that ring is the memory being written.
what gets inflamed by punishment: bitter is crimson, not gold. it spreads as a blotchy swelling over the mushroom body, jerks instead of pulsing, fades far more slowly — and the row that answered gets crossed out, because bitter weakens that row instead of strengthening it.
solid outlines are regions this model actually simulates. the dashed ones — optic lobes, lateral horn, central complex, subesophageal zone, taste organ — are anatomy it does not implement, and the last two light only because a real reward was delivered. no FlyWire data is used.

stimulate — 27 letters

this is the fly's entire vocabulary: A–Z plus space, 27 output cells and no more. everything the fly can say, it says from this row.

only A–Z and space can be answered. try typing a digit — the fly receives it, but has no way to say it back.

digits — the negative control

the fly was never trained on a digit and its output layer has no digit in it. these buttons are still real stimuli: the bitmap goes onto the same 35 receptors and the same circuit answers. it just cannot be right.

read a document, one letter at a time

the fly is shown one character at a time, in order, exactly as if you had pressed that letter's button — same 35 receptors, same circuit, same reward. the brain panel on the left animates for every character.

it can only read what its hand-drawn 5×7 font can draw: A–Z, 0–9 and space. digits are shown but cannot be answered — the fly has no output cell for them. punctuation and accented letters have no glyph at all, so they are stepped over. nothing is guessed, and nothing is claimed to have been read that was not.
